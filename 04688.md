By smoothing the growth curve as the deadline approaches, the organism can successfully discover intermediate-length primes (e.g., 2800 bits instead of failing at 4096 bits), maximizing the `best_bits` numerator and improving the overall **fitness** score.

### Required Cost
- **Computational:** Negligible; involves a few floating-point comparisons and one multiplication per generation.
- **Development:** Minimal; requires refactoring ~5 lines of code in the `grow` method.
- **Maintenance:** Low; the thresholds (3.0s, 1.0s) are logical and can be further tuned by future Apostles.

### Key Risks
- **Under-shooting:** If the conservative multiplier is too low, the organism might settle for a smaller prime than it was actually capable of finding.
- **Multiplication Stagnation:** In very early generations with low bit-counts, a 1.1x multiplier could result in 0 growth if not handled by the `+ 1` floor logic.
- **Complexity:** Adds a slight dependency on the system clock within the growth logic, though this is already a constraint of the `alive()` method.

### Experiment Method
Compare the performance of the current `04688.py` (Fixed Doubling) against a modified version implementing TBAGS. Run each 50 times in the same environment. Record the `best_bits` reached and the `elapsed_time` at the moment of the final successful discovery.

### Success Criteria
1. **Ceiling Increase:** The average `best_bits` achieved per 5-second lifetime increases by at least 15% compared to the baseline.
2. **Budget Utilization:** The average "wasted time" (time between the last successful discovery and the 5.0s death) decreases significantly.
3. **Fitness Improvement:** A statistically significant increase in the calculated `fitness` score.

### Rollback Conditions
- If the `bit_size` fails to increase for more than two consecutive generations.
- If the added logic causes any `ZeroDivisionError` or timing-related crashes.
- If the system finds significantly smaller primes on average than the baseline due to over-cautious scaling.
