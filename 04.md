## Candidate Name

### Core Change
Modify the `PrimeOrganism.make_candidate` method to implement an internal "Fast-Sieve" rejection loop. This change delivers a dual-stage performance explosion. First, it exponentially increases **Fitness** by ensuring that `total_attempts` (the denominator) only counts "high-quality" candidates that have already bypassed ~88% of potential composite values. Second, it dramatically improves **Execution Speed** at high bit-sizes; since Miller-Rabin's modular exponentiation is computationally expensive (O(k³)), skipping 88% of these calls allows the organism to attempt significantly larger bit-lengths (Ceiling Growth) within the same 5-second window.

- **Computational:** Negligible. Performing 168 modulo operations on a candidate is several orders of magnitude faster than a single Miller-Rabin round.
- **Memory:** Minimal. A static tuple of 168 integers (~1.3 KB).
- **Maintenance:** Zero. The list of primes under 1,000 is mathematically constant.

### Key Risks
- **Latency in `make_candidate`**: For very small bit-sizes (e.g., 16-bit), the probability of hitting a small prime is high, potentially causing the internal `while` loop to run many times. However, for the target growth (doubling bits), this overhead becomes invisible compared to the savings.
- **Search Bias**: The sieve only excludes known composites, so it does not introduce any bias that would prevent finding legitimate primes.

### Experiment Method
Execute the organism with the modified `make_candidate` against the current 04.py baseline. Monitor the `total_attempts` per successful prime discovery and the maximum `bit_size` reached.

### Success Criteria
1. **Fitness Multiplier**: At least a 5x increase in the final fitness score.
2. **Throughput**: A significant reduction in the number of Miller-Rabin modular exponentiation calls per generation.
3. **Ceiling Growth**: Reaching at least one bit-size generation further than the baseline (e.g., finding a 4096-bit or 8192-bit prime where the baseline stalled).

### Rollback Conditions
- If the `elapsed_time` for finding a 16-bit prime increases by more than 50% (indicating sieve overhead is too high for small integers).

This change directly addresses the "Efficiency" and "Discovery Speed" components of the Core Objective. By filtering the vast majority of composites with negligible CPU cost, the organism will drastically increase its "Candidate Throughput." This allows the system to evaluate significantly more candidates within the 5-second window, increasing the probability of discovering larger primes and substantially boosting the Fitness score (by reducing the `total_attempts` required to find a success).

### Required Cost
Minimal. The addition of 168 pre-computed integers has a negligible memory footprint (~1.3KB) and adds zero architectural complexity. It utilizes existing logic, requiring only a data update.

### Key Risks
There is a theoretical risk of "diminishing returns" where the time spent on trial division could exceed the time saved on Miller-Rabin for very small bit-sizes. However, for the target growth pattern (doubling from 16 to 2048+ bits), the cost of modular exponentiation quickly becomes orders of magnitude more expensive than 168 modulo operations.

1.  A measurable increase in the `total_attempts` (throughput) by at least 200%.
2.  A higher average `best_bits` (ceiling growth) compared to the baseline.
3.  A fitness score improvement of at least 2x.

### Rollback Conditions
