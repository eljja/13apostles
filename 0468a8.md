### [0] Adaptive Bit-Depth Backoff (ABDB)
```markdown
## Candidate Name
Adaptive Bit-Depth Backoff (ABDB)

### Core Change
Modify `PrimeOrganism.grow()` to incorporate a "stagnation tracker." If `search_one_generation()` returns `False` (fails to find a prime) for two consecutive growth attempts at a specific bit-depth, the system will trigger a `bit_size = int(bit_size * 0.9)` adjustment rather than continuing to grow or stall. This enables the organism to backtrack to a "discovery-viable" bit-size range instead of wasting the remainder of the 5-second lifetime on an impossible bit-size target.

### Expected Impact
This maximizes the **Efficiency** and **Ceiling Growth** components. Currently, the "Doubling Wall" often causes the organism to spend its entire final budget attempting to find a prime at a bit-size that is mathematically or statistically unreachable within the remaining seconds. Backing off allows the organism to find *another* (albeit slightly smaller) prime, ensuring the fitness function numerator (`best_bits`) captures a success rather than zeroing out due to timeout.

### Required Cost
- **Computational:** Negligible (two integer comparisons and one floating-point multiplication per generation).
- **Development:** Minimal (changing ~5 lines in `grow` and adding one state variable to `__init__`).
- **Maintenance:** Near zero; the logic is self-contained and easily auditable.

### Key Risks
- **Oscillation:** If the backoff logic is too aggressive, it could oscillate between two bit-sizes.
- **Mitigation:** The `stagnation_tracker` resets only on successful prime discovery, and the backoff is only triggered by *consecutive* failures, preventing rapid flipping.

### Experiment Method
Run 50 simulations with the current `0468a8.py` and 50 simulations with `ABDB`. Compare the number of lifetimes that end with a `None` result (failure to find any prime in the final generation) and the average `best_bits` score.

### Success Criteria
1. **Completion Rate:** A reduction in the number of generations that terminate without a prime discovery.
2. **Ceiling Utilization:** An increase in the `best_bits` value because the system settles at the largest *discoverable* prime rather than timing out at the largest *attempted* bit-size.
3. **Efficiency:** A net increase in the `success_count` per organism lifetime.

### Rollback Conditions
- If the organism spends more than 50% of its time re-testing previously discovered (smaller) bit-sizes.
- If the system encounters any `ZeroDivisionError` or invalid bit-size (e.g., `< 16`).
- If the `fitness` score does not show a statistically significant improvement over 50 test runs.
```

This evolution directly addresses the "Doubling Wall" blind spot. By transitioning from a rigid doubling strategy to a telemetry-aware growth model, the organism avoids spending its final, most valuable seconds on a search space it cannot statistically clear. This maximizes the **Useful Adaptation** goal by ensuring the `best_bits` numerator in the fitness function is as high as possible, rather than resulting in a "0" for the final generation due to a timeout. - **Maintenance**: Low. The logic is self-correcting and depends on the existing telemetry framework.

- **Under-reaching**: If the "safety buffer" (the `* 4` multiplier) is too conservative, the organism might settle for a 2000-bit prime when it could have potentially found a 4000-bit prime.

- If the organism fails to grow (bit_size remains static) for more than two generations.
