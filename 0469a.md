```markdown
## Candidate Name
Predictive Time-Bounded Growth Scaling (PTGS)

### Core Change
Modify the `PrimeOrganism` class to replace the static `GROWTH_FACTOR` with a dynamic growth calculation. The `grow` method will now use the `elapsed_time` of the most recently completed generation and the remaining time budget to estimate the maximum feasible `bit_size` for the next generation. Using the heuristic that Miller-Rabin complexity scales approximately with $O(\text{bits}^4)$ (incorporating both the modular exponentiation cost and the decreasing density of primes), the system will calculate a growth ratio that targets finding the next prime using only 50% of the remaining lifetime.

### Expected Impact
This addresses the "Terminal Generation Failure" blind spot. Currently, the organism often doubles its `bit_size` in the final second of life, moving from a range where discovery takes 0.5s to a range where it might take 4.0s. This results in a "suicidal" final generation that consumes the remaining budget without updating `best_bits`, crashing the fitness score. PTGS ensures the organism "downshifts" its growth as it approaches its 5-second mortality, maximizing the final `best_bits` numerator and ensuring every second of the lifecycle contributes to useful adaptation.

### Required Cost
- **Computational:** Negligible; involves a single floating-point power calculation ($x^{0.25}$) once per generation.
- **Development:** Low; requires adding a `last_duration` tracker and updating the `grow()` method logic.
- **Maintenance:** Minimal; the complexity heuristic is a fundamental property of primality testing.

### Key Risks
- **Heuristic Noise:** If a prime is found exceptionally quickly due to luck (low `attempts`), the system might over-estimate its ability and jump to a `bit_size` that is too large.
- **Under-shooting:** Conservative estimates might lead to smaller bit-size increases than the hardware is actually capable of handling.

### Experiment Method
Run the organism for 50 lifecycles. Compare the "Budget Utilization Efficiency"—the ratio of (Time spent in successful generations) to (Total 5.0s budget)—against the `0469a.py` baseline.

### Success Criteria
1. **Ceiling Optimization:** The average `best_bits` achieved at death increases by at least 10%.
2. **Success Count:** The average number of successful generations per lifetime increases, as the system "squeezes" in more reachable targets at the end.
3. **Fitness Stability:** A reduction in the variance of the fitness score across different runs.

### Rollback Conditions
- If the system enters an infinite loop or `ValueError` due to non-positive time calculations.
- If the `bit_size` ceases to grow (growth ratio stays $\leq 1.0$) despite having more than 1 second remaining.
- If the average `best_bits` falls below the baseline performance.
```
