## Candidate Name
Feedback-Driven Elastic Bit-Scaling (FEBS)

### Core Change
Refactor the `PrimeOrganism.grow()` method to replace the static, hardcoded time thresholds (0.6, 0.2) and multipliers (2.0, 1.4, 1.1) with a dynamic velocity-based calculation. 

The new logic will:
1.  Track the `time_spent` and `attempts_made` specifically for the most recent generation.
2.  Calculate the "Computational Cost per Bit" ($C = \text{time\_spent} / (\text{current\_bits} - \text{previous\_bits})$).
3.  Project the maximum achievable bit-size within the `remaining_time` based on the observed $C$.
4.  Set the next `bit_size` to a target that targets the 5.0s deadline as the "finish line" for the final discovery, rather than jumping in fixed increments.

### Expected Impact
This change addresses a critical blind spot in the current TBAGS (Time-Budgeted Adaptive Growth Scaling) logic: its inability to sense the difficulty of the search space. 
- **Ceiling Growth**: By calculating a trajectory based on real-time performance, the organism can "stretch" its bit-size more aggressively when discovery is fast and decelerate precisely when it hits computational resistance, maximizing the `best_bits` numerator.
- **Efficiency**: It prevents "overshooting"—where the organism jumps to a bit-size that is mathematically impossible to solve within the remaining 0.5 seconds—thereby reducing wasted `total_attempts` in the fitness denominator.
- **System Balance**: It transitions the growth strategy from a "blind pilot" (fixed timers) to a "sensor-fused navigator" (performance-aware).

### Required Cost
- **Computational**: Negligible. Requires storing two additional floats (`last_discovery_time` and `last_bit_size`) and performing basic arithmetic once per generation.
- **Development**: Minimal (~10-15 lines of code).
- **Maintenance**: Low; it makes the system more self-regulating and less dependent on "magic numbers" tuned to specific hardware.

### Key Risks
- **Under-sampling**: If the first 16-bit prime is found instantly, the "velocity" might be calculated as near-infinite, causing an unrealistic jump.
- **Mitigation**: Implement a "damping factor" and a maximum multiplier (e.g., cap at 2.0x) to ensure the first few generations remain stable.
- **Non-linear Complexity**: Prime testing time ($O(k^3)$ or $O(k^4)$) does not scale linearly with bit-size.
- **Mitigation**: The scaling logic should use a conservative projection (e.g., target 80% of projected bits) to account for the super-linear increase in cost.

### Experiment Method
Run the organism for 50 lifetimes. Record the `bit_size` of the final successful discovery in each run. Compare the "Success-to-Deadline Gap" (the time remaining after the final prime is found) between FEBS and the current TBAGS. A narrower gap combined with higher `best_bits` indicates superior budget utilization.

### Success Criteria
1.  **Increased Ceiling**: Average `best_bits` across 50 runs increases by $>15\%$.
2.  **Reduced Waste**: A $>20\%$ reduction in `total_attempts` spent on generations that fail to find a prime before the deadline.
3.  **Consistency**: Reduced variance in the `elapsed_time` recorded at the end of life (meaning the organism more consistently finishes close to the 5.0s limit).

### Rollback Conditions
- If the organism fails to find more than 2 primes in a lifetime due to over-aggressive jumps.
- If the `grow()` function introduces a `ZeroDivisionError` or `OverflowError`.
- If the `best_bits` average drops below the baseline `04688b7.py` average.
