## Deterministic Sieve-Jump Arithmetic (DSJA)

### Core Change

4.  Because $gcd(n, P) = 1 \implies gcd(n + kP, P) = 1$, every subsequent candidate is mathematically guaranteed to pass the low-level sieve without any additional computation or modular checks.

### Expected Impact
This is a paradigm shift that eliminates the "Quality Gate" overhead entirely. 
- **Discovery Speed**: The `while self.alive()` loop in `make_candidate`, which currently suffers from a high "SieveRatio" rejection rate (rejection of ~90% of candidates), is replaced by a single addition operation.

- **Ceiling Growth**: By spending zero CPU cycles on composite filtering after the first successful seed, the organism can dedicate nearly 100% of its 5-second window to the Miller-Rabin tests, allowing it to reach much higher bit-size ceilings.

### Required Cost
- **Computational**: Near zero. Replaces a loop of random generation and GCD calls with a single large-integer addition.
- **Development**: Minimal. Requires adding a `self.current_seed` attribute and refactoring `make_candidate` (approx. 10 lines).
- **Maintenance**: Low; requires a simple boundary check to ensure the `current_seed` does not exceed the target `bit_size`.

### Key Risks
- **Reduced Entropy**: By searching linearly from a random start point rather than sampling the entire bit-space, the organism might theoretically enter a "prime desert." However, the density of primes (via the Prime Number Theorem) suggests that the speed gain from jumping will far outweigh the risks of local clustering.
- **Bit-Length Drift**: Repeatedly adding `SIEVE_PRODUCT` will eventually increase the bit-length.
- **Mitigation**: The `search_one_generation` loop naturally resets when a prime is found or when the generation "grows," ensuring a fresh random seed for each bit-size.
