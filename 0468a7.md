## Candidate Name
Streaming Segmented Sieve (SSS)

### Core Change
Replace the stochastic "Generate-and-Check" candidate selection with a **Streaming Segmented Sieve** paradigm. 

Instead of generating a completely new random integer and performing an expensive `math.gcd` check for every attempt, the `PrimeOrganism` will:
1.  Pick a random starting base $S$ of the target bit-length.
2.  Initialize a boolean "sieve window" (e.g., a bitset or `bytearray`) of size $W = 2048$, representing the odd integers $\{S, S+2, S+4, \dots, S+2W-2\}$.
3.  For *every* prime $p$ in `SIEVE_DATA_CONSTANTS`, calculate the starting offset $O = (-S \pmod p)$ and mark all indices $k$ where $(2k \equiv O \pmod p)$ as composite.
4.  Yield candidates only from the indices that remain "True" in the sieve window.
5.  When the window is exhausted, pick a new random $S$ and repeat.

### Expected Impact
This introduces a **high-efficiency candidate pipeline**. 
*   **Efficiency:** Replaces thousands of repetitive `math.gcd` calls with a single linear sweep per window. This eliminates the "small factor" check overhead for all 168+ primes in `SIEVE_DATA_CONSTANTS` simultaneously.
*   **Discovery Speed:** The quality of candidates reaching the Miller-Rabin test increases drastically. By pre-filtering all small factors up to 997 (not just the first 32), the "hit rate" for primality tests will improve by an order of magnitude.
*   **Ceiling Growth:** By spending significantly less time in the candidate generation loop, the organism can dedicate more of its 5.0s budget to modular exponentiations at much higher bit-depths.

### Required Cost
- **Computational:** Low. A one-time sieve sweep per 2048 candidates is computationally cheaper than 2048 individual `math.gcd` calls.
- **Development:** Moderate. Requires restructuring the `make_candidate` logic into a generator-style state machine within the `PrimeOrganism`.
- **Maintenance:** Low. The `SIEVE_DATA_CONSTANTS` are already provided in the code.

### Key Risks
- **Local Clustering:** Searching in a window makes the search "local" around the starting base $S$. However, $S$ is randomized every 2048 attempts, maintaining global coverage.
- **Python Loop Overhead:** If the sieve window is too small, the overhead of the sieving loop might outweigh the gains.
- **Memory:** A `bytearray(2048)` is negligible (2KB).

### Experiment Method

### Success Criteria
1.  **Throughput:** At least a 3x increase in the number of candidates that pass the pre-sieve and reach the Miller-Rabin stage.
2.  **Ceiling Growth:** Reaching a higher maximum `bit_size` within the same 5.0s limit.
3.  **Fitness:** A measurable improvement in the fitness score due to reduced `total_attempts` and time spent per discovery.

### Rollback Conditions
