If time is extremely scarce, it will attempt a "Micro-Growth" ($+128$ bits) to ensure the final seconds of the lifecycle result in a successful discovery rather than a timeout.

### Expected Impact
This addresses the "Dead-End Generation" blind spot. Currently, the organism often spends the final 60–80% of its life searching for a prime at a bit-size that is computationally impossible to find before the `time_limit_sec` expires. By smoothing the growth curve based on real-time hardware performance, we maximize the `best_bits` numerator and minimize wasted `elapsed_time` in the fitness function. This ensures the system remains in its "computational sweet spot" for as long as possible.

- **Development:** Low. Requires modifying the `grow` signature and the `live` orchestration loop.
- **Maintenance:** Minimal. The cubic growth heuristic is a stable mathematical property of the Miller-Rabin test.

### Key Risks
- **Heuristic Pessimism:** If the time estimation is too conservative, the organism might "crawl" with small increments when it could have safely doubled, leading to a lower `best_bits`.
- **Complexity:** Introduces a dependency between the `search_one_generation` outcome and the `grow` logic, making the growth sequence non-deterministic across different hardware.
